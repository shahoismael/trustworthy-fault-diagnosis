## Abstract

In the field of industrial production, machine failures not only negatively affect productivity and product quality, but also lead to safety accidents, so it is crucial to accurately diagnose machine failures in time and take appropriate measures. However, machines cannot operate with faults for extended periods, and the diversity of fault modes results in limited data collection, posing challenges to building accurate fault prediction models. Despite recent advancements, intelligent fault diagnosis methods based on traditiona sampling and machine learning have shown notable progress. Nonetheless, these methods heavily rely on human expertise, making it challenging to extract comprehensive feature information. To address these challenges, numerous imbalance fault diagnosis methods based on generative adversarial networks (GANs) have emerged, GANs can generate realistic samples that conform to the distribution of the original data, showing promising results in diagnosing imbalances in critical components such as bearings and gears, despite their great potential, GAN methods also face challenges, including difficulties in training and generating abnormal samples. However, whether it is GAN-based resampling technology or traditional sampling technology, there are fewer reviews on noise-containing imbalance, intra- and inter-class dual imbalance, multi-class imbalance, time series imbalance and other problems in small samples, and there is a lack of a more comprehensive summary of the solutions to the above imbalance problems. Therefore, the purpose of this paper is to deeply explore the imbalance problems under various failure modes, and review and analyze the research methods and results based on GANs on this basis. By suggesting future research directions, this paper aims to provide guidance and reference for research in the field of industrial production maintenance.

Keywords: generative adversarial networks, fault diagnosis, complex data imbalance, small samples, data generation

## 1. Introduction

‘Germany’s Industry Strategy 2030’ underscores Germany’s con viction that the pivotal breakthrough innovation lies in digitalization, particularly the application of artificial intelligence (Germann, 2023). China’s ‘14th Five-Year Plan and 2035 Vision Outline’ emphasizes a focus on artificial intelligence algorithms, advocating for the profound integration of intelligent manufac turing across various industries to propel the manufacturing sec tor towards high-end, intelligent, and environmentally friendly advancements. In the evolving global industrial landscape, mechanical equipment faces the challenge of meeting demands fo heightened efficiency, precision, and managing intricate produc tion tasks, all amid dynamic and complex operational conditions that frequently lead to machine failures and safety incidents (Han et al., 2020). Consequently, the significance of condition monitor ing and fault diagnosis in machinery and equipment cannot be overstated, as they are crucial for preventing major accidents and improving production efficiency. The primary objective of fault diagnosis is to establish a mapping relationship between machine monitoring data and diverse health conditions, ensuring the smooth operation of machinery and equipment (Yang et al., 2019). Conventional fault diagnosis techniques prove inefficient due to their dependence on a priori knowledge and manual processing, making it challenging to extract features manually for analyz ing faults in complex, highly automated modern machinery and equipment (Xing et al., 2022).

The application of machine learning methods, such as Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) (Han & Kim, 2024), support vector machines (SVMs) (Goyal et al., 2020), and deep neural networks (Lin et al., 2024), has significantly reduced the influence of human factors in fault diagnosis. Deep learning-based fault diagnosis techniques find extensive applications in the processing of machinery big data, facilitated by the widespread deployment of low-cost sensors and internet connectivity (Zhao et al., 2019). This trend establishes a prerequisite for data-driven intelligent fault diagnosis methods, with many evolving deep learning techniques consistently achieving commendable results in machine health monitoring. However, for deep learning models to demonstrate effective and accurate fault diagnosis results in realworld scenarios post-training, a substantial number of categories and a balanced distribution of sample sizes are crucial during model training. In practical scenarios, the gradual process of abnormalities or failures in key components of mechanical systems (Yýlmaz & Sýnmazçelik, 2007), limits the data collected during abnormal states, while normal operational states easily yield copious amounts of data, resulting in a data imbalance issue (Lei et al., 2020). Data imbalance, where the quantity of collected fault data is significantly lower than normal samples, introduces model bias in data-driven fault diagnosis methods (Shen et al., 2023). Recent years have witnessed numerous approaches to address imbalanced classification, focusing on designing classification methods that simultaneously ensure accuracy for both majority and minority classes within data samples. These approaches operate at three main levels: data preprocessing (Lin et al., 2018), feature extraction (Maldonado et al., 2014), and classifier improvement (Batuwita & Palade, 2010). The research advancements in feature extraction and classifier improvement lag behind those in data preprocessing. Feature extraction encompasses several notable methods, including the K-OFSD (K-nearest neighbors Online Feature Selection) algorithm proposed by Zhou et al. (2017), designed to enhance separability between majority and minority class samples.

Additionally, Maldonado et al. introduced a scaling factor tech nology as an embedded strategy for feature selection (Maldon ado & López, 2018). In terms of classifier improvement, Khan et al. presented a robust feature representation cost-sensitive deep neural network capable of autonomously learning both majority and minority classes (Khan et al., 2018). Wong et al. proposed the Cost-Sensitive Deep Neural Network Ensemble (CSDE) (Wong et al., 2020), which concurrently employs random undersampling and layerwise feature extraction within the deep neural network’s hidden layer. These classifier improvement strategies primarily emphasize cost-sensitive and ensemble learning. At the data preprocessing level, researchers primarily manipulate the dataset balance by increasing (decreasing) the number of samples through resampling techniques for the minority (majority) class. Resampling techniques are broadly categorized into three groups: undersampling, oversampling, and mixed sampling (He & Garcia, 2009). The oversampling method improves the performance of the classification model by expanding the data, because when classifying imbalanced data, the classifier often tends to predict the majority class to satisfy the overall decreasing trend of error, and due to the insufficient number of samples in the minority class, the classifier is unable to adequately learn the features of the class, which leads to misclassification of samples in the minority class, by expanding the data, the classifier is able to better learn the features of the minority class, to reduce the bias and thus perform better on different sample distributions. Feature selection and weighted loss function methods can address imbalance issues without altering the data volume, but they have limitations. Feature selection can be complex and may result in information loss Similarly weighted loss functions require careful selection of weights and are sensitive to extreme imbalances, necessitating extensive experimental tuning and limiting their effectiveness in diverse imbalance scenarios. In contrast, data expansion through oversampling offers a more generalizable and adaptable solution effectively handling most imbalance-related challenges. Beyond standalone application, many approaches combine resampling algorithms with feature selection, classifier improvement, etc. Yuan et al. have provided an insightful summary of such integrated approaches (Yuan et al., 2023).

Compared to traditional data augmentation techniques, generative models like Variational Autoencoder Networks (VAEs) (Kingma & Welling, 2022) and generative adversarial networks (GANs; Goodfellow et al., 2020) can serve as effective tools for sample generation in data augmentation. Although the networkbased synthesis method is more intricate, the samples produced exhibit heightened realism and diversity. The recently popular Diffusion model has also been applied to imbalanced fault diagnosis. Yang et al. used the diffusion model to generate data prior to diagnosing imbalanced faults. However, this approach appears to be less suitable for small-sample scenarios, and its applicability has not yet been fully demonstrated (Yang et al., 2024). GAN, as a generative model employing adversarial training, demonstrates a potent capability for image generation, yielding more realistic samples. Through extensive research and iterative enhancements to the original GAN, its performance has significantly advanced. Numerous proposed GAN variants have further tailored its adaptability to various data types across diverse fields, as illustrated in Fig. 1, depicting the increasing number of articles related to GANs in the field of fault diagnosis since their inception. Several comprehensive reviews on the application of GAN networks in fault diagnosis exist, such as the examination of machine learning applications in machine fault diagnosis at three stages (Lei et al., 2020). Pan et al. categorized GAN-based intelligent fault diagnosis methods into three aspects: data augmentation, transfer learning, and anomaly detection (Pan et al., 2022). However, their work mainly introduces the applications and impacts of traditional machine learning, deep learning, and transfer learning, with limited details on GAN technology principles and methods. Brophy et al. delved into the evolution of GAN structure and loss function changes in time series data processing based on GANs (Brophy et al., 2022). Their comprehensive exploration encompassed the classification and methodologies for processing continuous and discrete time series using GANs, though it only briefly addressed a few industrial applications. Notably, their coverage of fault diagnosis methods based on GAN technology was not exhaustive. Additionally, scenarios involving noise imbalance, intra-class and inter-class imbalances, and multi-class imbalance, which align more closely with the diagnostic needs of industrial settings, were rarely addressed in these papers. Consequently, this paper conducts a comprehensive review of fault diagnosis algorithms based on GANs to offer insights into the application of GAN in fault diagnosis and propel the development of GAN technology.

The structure of this article is as follows: Section 2 offers a concise overview of GAN principles. Section 3 delves into the traits of intricate and imbalanced data. Section 4 provides a comprehensive description of GAN network’s technology for augmenting imbalanced data and its specific application in diagnosing imbalanced faults. In Section 5, we scrutinize challenges associated with GAN technology in various imbalance scenarios and explore research on interpretability and privacy protection concerning small sample sizes. Finally, Section 6 summarizes the overall framework of GAN technology in the realm of fault diagnosis, offering insights into the future development of GANs concerning uneven data.

## 2. Brief of GAN Principle

The management and maintenance of industrial equipment often lead to production stagnation, primarily due to untimely fault diagnosis and detection, which can potentially result in safety incidents. A fundamental challenge impeding fault monitoring and machine maintenance is the scarcity of fault samples. The imbalance between fault and normal data, especially in scenarios with limited samples, presents a significant obstacle to the development of precise intelligent diagnosis models. Additionally, variations in data characteristics, such as amplitude and fre quency, across different speeds and loads necessitate a comprehensive approach. Relying solely on individual samples is inadequate in an industrial context, as it fails to meet the demands of ensuring stable, secure, and efficient equipment operation amid changing conditions and complex structures. Constructing a fault diagnosis model requires accounting for several factors, including the presence of excess noise during equipment operation, the varying degree of equipment failure over time, the imbalance between samples and multiple classes due to disparities in damage among different components, and the imbalance resulting from the pronounced time series characteristics of mechanical faults.

Data in industrial environments typically exhibit imbalance and conventional classification models, such as the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) (Chua, 1998), which are optimized for overall classification accuracy, frequently exhibit bias towards majority classes. This bias can inadvertently neglect minority classes, which may hold greater research significance. Approaches to ad dress imbalanced classification primarily focus on data preprocessing, feature extraction, and classifier enhancement. Extensive research has been conducted on traditional imbalanced data processing methods, particularly in areas such as data processing, algorithm equalization, and ensemble learning. Equilibrium algorithms incorporate cost-sensitive learning into the decisionmaking process of base classifiers by adjusting the weight of misclassified minority samples, it is proposed to integrate this cost sensitive approach into CNN (Chung et al., 2015). Two prevalent methods for preprocessing data samples are oversampling and undersampling. The main idea is to balance the entire sample set by increasing the number of minority samples or taking away some samples from the majority class. Among these, oversampling methods play a prominent role, preserving crucial information compared to alternative methods. However, typical oversampling methods, including random oversampling (Bennin et al., 2018), synthetic minority oversampling (SMOTE) (Chawla et al., 2002), and ADASYN (Adaptive synthetic) (He et al., 2008), present challenges such as over-generalization, sample overlapping, noise introduction, and neglect of boundary samples. Wei et al. proposed a majority-weighted minority oversampling technique based on cohesive hierarchical clustering (Cluster-MWMOTE) (Wei et al., 2020), which can avoid ignoring the small-sized subset of minorities far away from the majority class instances, and achieve better results. However, fundamentally speaking, SMOTE is an interpolation-based strategy generating intermediate samples within the neighborhood of a few points in the sample space to expand positive samples, this method is criticized for its mechanical nature and its failure to adequately capture the holistic dis tribution of original samples.

However, in recent years, the sample generation methods based on GAN network have addressed these issues to some extent, and the GAN-based approach represents a specialized oversampling method from the perspective of data synthesis. The basic GAN model (Goodfellow et al., 2020) structure is shown in Fig. 2, comprises a generative model G (generator) and a discriminator model D (discriminator), which engage in a two-player zero-sum game where they compete. The purpose of generator G is to learn the probability distribution of data from the given data, which maps the input random noise into observational data. In contrast, the discriminator D aims to assess whether the data produced by generator G conforms to the probability distribution of real samples. This function is akin to a binary classifier that estimates the probability of the generated data being real, with values ranging from 0 to 1, where an ideal outcome would be a probability of 0.5 (Goodfellow et al., 2020). GAN has excellent performance in image synthesis, computer vision, natural language processing, and medical detection due to their robust ability to fit real data distribution (Hong et al., 2019). GAN-based resampling strategies can increase the number of high-similarity pseudo-samples, thereby optimiz ing the training process of fault diagnosis models and enhancing diagnostic accuracy. The training process based on the idea of GAN game adversarial is added to CNN as a regularization strategy (Han et al., 2019), so as to improve the generalization ability of the model.

## 3. Characteristic Analysis of Complex Imbalanced Data

## 3.1 Noise under small sample imbalance

Noisy samples often exhibit substantial differences from other normal samples. When the sample data categories are clear and pure, model training can achieve rapid convergence. However, the presence of noisy elements within the training samples significantly impairs training speed and accuracy, and in some cases, convergence may not be attained at all. Noise within smallsample imbalanced data manifests as random errors stemming from anomalous factors when sample size is limited, leading to imbalances in the available sample category proportions. This noise strongly interferes with few-shot learning. The BSMOTE (Borderline-SMOTE) technique introduced by Han et al. utilizes the k-NN (k-Nearest Neighbors) method to calculate Euclidean distance for determining the k nearest neighbors of a sample (Han et al., 2005). If the properties of the k nearest neighbors are unrelated to the sample, it is deemed noise within imbalanced data. While the k-NN method is versatile, improper k values may lead to the oversight or overestimation of noise, ignoring boundary samples or true noise. A noise processing method based on Euclidean distance, proposed by Lin et al., identifies samples with a larger Euclidean distance from their class center as true noise (Batuwita & Palade, 2010). However, this method is only suitable for spherical datasets. Alternatively, Ju et al. consider samples with both a larger Euclidean distance from their class center and a smaller density of k nearest neighbors belonging to the same class as noise (Ju et al., 2016). This combined approach of using Euclidean distance and neighbor density improves noise detection to a certain extent. The oversampling technique represented by K-means SMOTE (Douzas et al., 2018) utilizes clustering to partition noise. Specifically, if the number of majority class instances in a subcluster exceeds that of minority class instances, the minority class samples in the subcluster are classified as noise. ## 3.2 Intra-class and inter-class imbalances and multi-class imbalance in fault modes

The inter-class imbalance refers to the large difference in the amount of data among different categories, which is also a primary concern in the field of fault diagnosis. Intra-class imbalance occurs when a single class is distributed into multiple sub-clusters, each with varying amounts of data, which has been discussed in the literature (Japkowicz, 2001; Japkowicz & Stephen, 2002) and other papers. When a specific class of failure occurs in industrial machinery and equipment, the change of the speed of the shaft and the difference in load will lead to the class characteristics of the failure mode (Shen et al., 2021; An et al., 2022), which is manifested as point migration in the data space. The existence or absence of intra-class imbalance is related to the severity of the fault under the same fault state, under normal circumstances, faulty machines initially experience a higher frequency of minor faults, which may escalate to more severe faults due to cumulative effects. This escalation impacts the intelligent diagnosis of severe faults. Under complex working conditions, there will be simultaneous faults in multiple locations of the machinery and equipment, leading to the emergence of various sub-categories of faults. Some components may have a higher number of fault samples, while others have fewer. However, all these fault samples are significantly outnumbered by normal samples. The practical process often requires managing multiple minority and majority classes simultaneously, which leads to the multi-class imbalance in the industrial environment. The multi-class imbalance under the small sample is very prone to class overlap due to the large proportion of the number of minority classes to the majority class (Mukherjee et al., 2019), as shown in Fig. 4, which is a schematic of the intra-class imbalance and inter-class imbalance and multiclass imbalance, and it is easy to ignore some more important boundary samples during practical processing.

Within the realm of intelligent fault diagnosis models, the imbalance in vibration signal fault data significantly affects diagnostic accuracy. ## 4. GAN-based Imbalanced Data Enhancement Technology and Its Application in Fault Diagnosis

Since its inception, GANs have emerged as one of the most promising methodologies within the realm of deep learning. Operating as a model of unsupervised learning, GANs are widely applied in computer vision and image processing. Similarly, within the domain of mechanical fault diagnosis, GANs have demon strated remarkable performance. The adversarial learning framework of GANs enables them to generate data that closely resembles real data from a higher dimension. This capacity proves instrumental in optimizing imbalanced classification tasks, especially in scenarios with limited samples. Leveraging the GAN network to augment imbalanced data has proven effective in successfully addressing various fault diagnosis challenges in industrial contexts. This section systematically reviews and summarizes the principle of GAN network processing imbalanced data and its application in fault diagnosis of critical equipment and key components, covering journal and conference papers published in recent years.

## 4.1.1 GAN for unsupervised learning

The purpose of the original GAN (i.e., vanilla GAN) is to minimize the objective function, which is as follows:

$$
\begin{array}{r l} & {\min _ {G} \max _ {D} V (D, G) = \mathbb {E} _ {\mathbf {x} \sim p _ {\mathrm{data}} (\mathbf {x})} [ \log D (\mathbf {x}) ]} \\ & {\qquad + \mathbb {E} _ {\mathbf {z} \sim p _ {\mathbf {z}} (\mathbf {z})} [ \log (1 - D (G (\mathbf {z}))) ]} \end{array}\tag{3}
$$

where D(x) indicates the probability of discriminating the real data of the input discriminator as coming from the real sample, $1 - D ( G ( \mathbf { z } ) )$ indicates that the generated data of the input discriminator is discriminated as the probability from the generated sample, $p _ { \mathtt { d a t a } } ( \mathbf { x } )$ represents the probability distribution of the real sample, and $p _ { \mathbf { z } } ( \mathbf { z } )$ represents the probability distribution of random noise. For the generator (G), the objective is to minimize the probability of being detected as ‘fake’ by the discriminator (D). Conversely, the discriminator (D) aims to maximize the probability of correctly identifying data as ‘fake.’ In this adversarial game, G and D iteratively deceive and identify each other. Through this iterative process, both models continuously update their parameters via backpropagation. Ultimately, the data generated by the GAN closely approximates and aligns with the given real data samples.

Since the emergence of GAN, it has been accompanied by its own inherent training instability caused by gradient vanishing and pattern crashing, and the initial improvements have changed from minimizing JS divergence (Jensen-Shannon divergence) (Lin, 1991) to minimizing KL divergence (Kullback–Leibler divergence)

(Kullback & Leibler, 1951), that is, removing the $" \mathrm { 1 } "$ in Eq. (3), but the original convex optimization based on the space ${ \mathrm { V } } ( { \mathrm { D } } , G )$ of probability density functions cannot be proven to be convergent in the parameter space. In particular, when applying GAN-based methods to address the issue of small sample imbalance, the problem of mode collapse will be more serious, so numerous improvement strategies have been developed both to address inherent deficien cies in GANs and to improve the performance with small, imbal anced datasets The convolutional structure in DCGAN can better extract the features in the samples, improve the stability of the training process and the quality of the generated samples, the convolutional structure should be used to generate fusion data from multiple sensors (Liu et al., 2020), and the two-dimensiona convolution structure of DCGAN was changed (Wang et al., 2021b), and the one-dimensional convolution was used to better generate the timing data in the vibration signal. This replacement of the network structure was used as the basic structure in the subsequent GAN structure design, and it was widely used. VAEGAN originates from the similarity of the functions and structures of the decoder in VAE and the generator in GAN, and the origina data is compressed into the hidden space through the encoder, and a noise space to be sampled close to the center of the original sample space can provide a more reliable latent vector input for the GAN structure. The VAEGAN structure is used to realize the fault diagnosis of industrial time series (Jiang et al., 2019), and its encoder, decoder, and discriminator structures all use the convolutional network just mentioned, and interestingly, it is mentioned that a feature extractor is added before the entire GAN structure, so that the size of the input data can be reduced. In contrast, the sliding window sampling technique is used to oversample the original samples (Wang et al., 2023c), and then it is used as an adaptive strategy for the subsequent VAEGAN fault diagnosis tasks. Phase space reconstruction (RPS) is a novel form of data in the VAEGAN diagnostic strategy (Rathore & Harsha, 2023). Lei et al. (2024) combine VAEGAN and self-attention mechanism to improve the quality of generated samples. In addition, Shirshah et al. (2024) use VAEGAN to investigate the causal relationships between variables to identify the propagation paths and root causes of industrial plant failures. Sun et al. (2024) accomplish cross-domain data enhancement and fault diagnosis through VAEGAN while protecting data privacy.

Similar to VAEGAN, which maps real data back to the hidden space, BiGAN (Donahue et al., 2017) integrates the encoded vectors of real data with real data, and sends them into the discrim inator with the combination of pseudo-samples and noise vectors to form a two-way structure. BiGANs trained on only normal or healthy samples have shown promising results in diagnosing faults in industrial robots (Pu et al., 2022, 2023a), or accurately diagnose 3D printers (Li et al., 2021a). CycleGAN (Zhu et al., 2017) is used to learn the data mapping relationship between the two sample domains, and the cyclic consistency loss forces the generated samples to maintain the characteristics of the source domain data when learning the distribution of the tar get domain data. CycleGAN in reference (Zhao & Huang, 2021) is a good example in the fault diagnosis of rolling bearings; Cycle-GAN is used as an industrial fault data augmentation (Pu et al., 2023b), but it redesigns a new cyclic consistent generative ad versarial constraint; In order to maintain stable operating conditions and maintain fault consistency, two additional discriminators were added on the basis of CycleGAN (Zhao et al., 2022), so that the generated samples matched the target working conditions and retained domain invariant fault information. Addition ally, Guo et al. (2024b) used CycleGAN for defect detection in infrared thermography samples of imbalanced rolling bearings, and Qin et al. (2024) constructed a digital twin model based on the relationship between vibration response and measured signals. In addition to the above GAN variants, there are also GANs based on modules and structural improvements, such as reference (Zhang et al., 2021c) which separates the classifier of subordinate discrim inators in InfoGAN (Chen et al., 2016) and StytleGAN (Karras et al., 2019) for applications in the field of fault diagnosis (Wang et al., 2021a).

The modified GAN variant based on the module also shows the difference in the structural form of the loss function because of the change of the overall structure. However, there are some GAN variants that directly modify the loss function based on the GAN metric deficiencies. The classic WGAN (Wasserstein GAN) and WGAN-gp (Wasserstein GAN with gradient penalty) are based on the improvement of the loss function, and the distance between distributions is measured by the Wasserstein distance. In order to satisfy the Lipschitz continuity of the discriminator parameters, the WGAN clips the parameter weights, and its objective function is shown as follows:

$$
\begin{array}{r l} & {\min _ {G} \max _ {D \in \mathcal {L}} V (D, G) = \mathbb {E} _ {\mathbf {x} \sim p _ {\mathrm{data}} (\mathbf {x})} [ \log D (\mathbf {x}) ]} \\ & {\qquad - \mathbb {E} _ {\mathbf {z} \sim p _ {\mathbf {z}} (\mathbf {z})} [ \log (1 - D (G (\mathbf {z}))) ]} \end{array}\tag{4}
$$

where represents the 1-Lipschitz function, i.e., the discriminator should be subordinate to a set of 1-Lipschitz functions. WGAN-gp adds a gradient penalty term on the basis of WGAN, limiting the two norms of the entered gradient to around 1, and adding the penalty term $- \lambda \underset { \hat { x } P _ { \hat { v } } } { \mathrm { ~ F ~ } } [ ( \lVert \nabla _ { \hat { x } } D ( \hat { x } ) \rVert _ { 2 } - 1 ) ^ { 2 } ]$ directly after Eq. (4), which is the objective function of WGAN-gp. In the penalty term, λ represents the penalty coefficient, xˆ is a randomly sampled sample from the real data distribution and the generator distribution, D(xˆ)is the gradient of the discriminator to $\hat { x } ,$ and represents the L norm.

Using WGAN, Fan et al. (2022) and Wan et al. (2021) extended the application to small sample data for rolling bearings, which significantly improved the accuracy of fault diagnosis. The enhanced minimum gating unit is integrated into the WGAN generator and discriminator in order to obtain latent information from the temporal data of the streaming industry (Zhang et al., 2023b), and the trained stacked autoencoder is used to output the required noise vectors to obtain high-quality samples before proposing the generator at WGAN (Rathore & Harsha, 2022). WGAN-gp is also widely used: there is a simple application of WGAN-gp (Shang et al., 2023), while a new discriminator is designed by using spectral normalization (SN) (Zhang et al., 2020a), and the general fault diagnosis is based on the vibration signal to achieve feature recognition, while it utilizes a WGAN-GP-based model to complete the diagnostic task using current signals (Li et al., 2021b), it first applies a filtering operation to the input electrical signals.

Compared to the original GAN, WGAN and WGAN-GP, with their modified loss functions, show substantial improvements in training stability and the quality of generated pseudo-samples. This suggests that combining loss function enhancements with modular improvements can further advance fault diagnosis. For instance, integrating the loss functions of VAEGAN and WGAN has been utilized to achieve more accurate fault diagnosis (Liu et al., 2021a; Wang et al., 2023b). Additionally, the Wasserstein distance has demonstrated superior performance when employed in CycleGAN (Pu et al., 2023a). Combining the loss functions in VAEGAN and WGAN-GP has been shown to enhance fault diagnosis capabilities (Miao et al., 2022; Guo et al., 2024a). Beyond these improvements to the loss function, some studies have adopted the LSGAN method, which utilizes the least squares loss function instead of the traditional GAN loss function. This approach not only enhances training stability but also facilitates fault diagnosis in planetary drive scenarios (Zhang et al., 2024b).

## 4.1.2 GAN for supervised learning

The conditional generative adversarial network (CGAN) (Mirza & Osindero, 2014) modifies the training methodology of the original GAN by incorporating conditional constraints through additional label information, rather than relying solely on random noise. This approach guides the model to generate target data by conditioning the input layer of both the generator and discriminator with the stitched labels. Consequently, the CGAN model is capable of producing controllable and specific samples. The objective function of CGAN is shown below;

$$
\begin{array}{r l} & {\min _ {G} \max _ {D} V (D, G) = \mathbb {E} _ {\mathbf {x} \sim p _ {\mathrm{data}} (\mathbf {x})} [ \log D (\mathbf {x} | \mathbf {y}) ]} \\ & {\qquad + \mathbb {E} _ {\mathbf {z} \sim p _ {2} (\mathbf {z})} [ \log (1 - D (G (\mathbf {z} | \mathbf {y}))) ]} \end{array}
$$

where x y and z y represents the real data and noise input under condition y, respectively.

Based on the DCGAN structure and combined with the category labels, Luo et al. (2021) and Peng et al. (2022a) successfully capture the true distribution of the original vibration data using the CDC-GAN model. Similarly, the CGAN model can be applied to amplify a small number of fault samples to efficiently and accurately detect the short-circuit fault of the motor (Li et al., 2021e). Additionally, combining CGAN with empirical mode decomposition methods enables fault identification in the distribution networks (Hou et al., 2022). Generally speaking, condition-based GANs represent a specialized modification of the modular structure, which introduces class-specific losses. By integrating various improvements with other structures and loss functions, higher-quality fault samples can be generated. Introducing the idea of trend diffusion theory into the CGAN model enhances its performance in data augmentation and get more effective diagnosis of submersible electric pumps with extremely limited fault data (Gao et al., 2024b). For the fault diagnosis of chillers, the introduction of Wasserstein distance into CGAN makes the training data diversified and more balanced (Yan et al., 2020), which effectively improves the fault diagnosis performance of chillers. Some methods adapt CGAN to the loss function of WGAN-gp (Li et al., 2022c; Yan et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2022: Gu et al., 2023: Chen et al, 2024: Zhang et al., 2024c). thereby greatly improving diagnostic performance. In order to increase the diversity of samples and alleviate the collapse of the model, Li et al. (2023b) added a loss term based on the pseudosample entropy increase model on the basis of WGAN-gp, forcing the generator to learn diversity. VAEGAN and CGAN were used in combination for the fault diagnosis of gearboxes (Wang et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2022e; Zhang et al., 2022b). In addition to the above improvement strategies, there are also models that combine D2GAN with CGAN (Zheng et al., 2020).

## 4.1.3 GAN for semi-supervised learning

The Semi-Supervised GAN (SSGAN) utilizes a discriminator that functions as a multi-classifier, distinguishing between real sample classes and a special class of pseudo-samples, with SSGAN exclusively accepting label information. This approach has been applied to rotating machinery fault diagnosis by combining wavelet transform and SSGAN to label a limited number of samples (Liang, Deng, Wu, Li et al., 2020a). And a method integrated clustering algorithms and SSGAN, merging unsupervised and semi-supervised learning techniques to effectively identify unlabeled faults (Li & Zhang, 2023). An improved actor-critic algorithm is combined with SSGAN to diagnose faults when the annotation sample size is small (Wang et al., 2022b). Furthermore, there is a type of semi supervised learning of GANs, in which the discriminator of the DCGAN is changed to a multi-classifier (Zhou et al., 2023), which can be trained using limited labeled data and a large amount of inexpensive unlabeled data.

ACGAN (Odena et al., 2017) extends CGAN by incorporating an auxiliary classifier within the discriminator, which simultaneously outputs category information while distinguishing between real and fake samples. Unlike CGAN, the input of ACGAN does not need to splice samples with labels, but the auxiliary classifier of the discriminator needs to learn the category information of the input samples through the added category labels. In fact, ACGAN can be viewed as a hybrid of CGAN and SSGAN, which combines a generator that generates labeled samples in a supervised manner and a discriminator that discriminates authenticity unsupervised, so it is also a kind of GANs with semi-supervised learning. Some studies directly applied ACGAN in their modeling strategies to enhance minority class samples and address class imbalance (Fu et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024c). An adaptive strategy was de signed to establish a fault diagnosis model for rotating machinery using the ACGAN model (Li et al., 2019). Shao et al. (2019) and Guo et al. (2020) use the ACGAN model of one-dimensional convolutional structure to achieve accurate diagnosis of mechanical faults. Meng et al. (2022) and Dai et al. (2023a) combine ACGAN with Wasserstein distance loss, while Li et al. (2021c) combine AC-GAN with WGAN-gp. Further improvements to the ACGAN structure include the design of dual auxiliary classifiers (He et al., 2020), the separation of the auxiliary classifiers from the original AC-GAN discriminator to improve classification performance (Li et al., 2022b), and the creation of multiple generators with shared local weights (Guo et al., 2022a) to capture more distribution patterns. Additionally, incorporating coral loss terms has been shown to increase the diversity of generated samples, as demonstrated by experiments in this study. Moreover, some methods have changed and designed ACGAN into a supervised mode. For instance, Dixit et al. (2021) improved the classification accuracy by adding redun dant label information combined with meta-learning methods.

By integrating the basic models of various GANs or implementing other targeted improvements, the performance of GANs can be greatly improved, especially the quality of generated samples, which is conducive to improving the classification accuracy of classification models in the future. For different learning tasks and data forms, it is necessary to choose different objective functions or module structures and learning paradigms, and the constructed GAN models need to be further optimized and adjusted to adapt to a variety of input forms. Although the overall structure or principle of GAN models remains consistent across various tasks, the specific details can vary. ## 4.2 Methods for GANs to handle imbalanced data across various data forms

Fault diagnosis in the industrial domain relies on diverse data sources, including vibration signals from sensors, temperature and humidity readings, current and voltage signals, sound signals, log information recorded by equipment and system operations, as well as image and video data captured by cameras or detected by vision sensors.

These data are presented in various formats and are typically processed either as time series or images during fault diagnosis. They are analyzed for tasks such as fault type classification, anomaly detection, and life prediction. Time-domain data is predominant in industrial fault analysis; however, time series data can be transformed into the frequency domain or time-frequency domain using techniques such as Fast Fourier Transform (FFT), Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT), Wavelet Transform (WT). Moreover, statistical features can be extracted from time series, or the data can be converted into image formats for subsequent use as input. ## 4.2.1 Approaches used by GANs for handling time series and frequency domain data

The generation of time series involves not only learning the data distribution at each time point to capture static features but also understanding the autocorrelation within the series and real-time features. Time series data can be categorized into discrete time series and continuous time series. Discrete time series may en counter scenarios where the gradient is zero at certain points, whereas continuous time series face challenges in performance evaluation due to the long-term dependencies within the data and the lack of standardized dimensions (Brophy et al., 2022). Data obtained from sensors originates from a continuous physical pro cess and thus represents continuous data. However, computers represent it as a discrete time series through discrete time-step sampling and computation. Subsequently, time series data can be transformed into frequency domain data via Fast Fourier Transform. In dealing with time series, GANs provide various structures for selection in their generators and discriminators.

## Linear neural network

A simple linear neural network can process time series by inputting sequence data into the linear network, mapping to each neuron, and reconstructing the original distribution through adversarial learning. For example, a fully connected structure is used to directly process the original time series (Zhang, Chen, Li et al., 2021c), the time series is converted into a series of numbers in the frequency domain (Wang et al., 2020), and then the frequency domain data is generated through a dense connection structure, which is also used to generate frequency domain data, and multilayer perceptrons are used in some references (Zheng et al., 2020; Li et al., 2022c).

## One-dimensional convolutional neural network

Convolutional neural networks are initially used to deal with twodimensional data forms such as pictures, specifically, the picture is a HxWnumerical matrix, the idea of dealing with time series is to consider it as a 1xWultra-long matrix, and the convolution kernel becomes the size of 1xN. After defining the step size and padding size, a one-dimensional convolutional kernel can be used to slide on the sequence and perform convolutional computation like a transparent window. This process extracts feature maps from the sequence. Subsequent outputs are then obtained through pooling, activation, and other related operations. A onedimensional inverse convolution is used as the main structure of the generator (Wang et al., 2021b), while the discriminator uses a one-dimensional convolutional structure. Both the generator and the discriminator use one-dimensional convolution (Shao et al., 2019), except that an upsampling operation is needed in the gen erator, and a similar construction is used in other scenarios (Guo et al., 2020).

## Two-dimensional convolutional neural network

Convolutional neural networks were initially used for feature extraction from images, where matrix data in the form of images could fully leverage the capabilities of CNNs. A possible approach for time series analysis is to reshape the one-dimensional time series into a two-dimensional form. This involves reshaping a time series of length M<sup>2</sup> into a matrix of size MxM (Li et al., 2021c; Miao et al., 2022), the processing can be continued using a twodimensional convolutional network in the discriminator. On the contrary, the two-dimensional feature matrix obtained from the noise mapping in the generator needs to be spread out to the length of the original sample and then output.

## Recurrent neural networks

Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) excel at processing sequence data. Their cyclic structure allows the network to maintain a memory state while processing each sequence element and pass that memory state to the next element. This memory mechanism enables RNNs to capture temporal information in sequences. Long short-term memory networks (LSTMs) and gated recurrent units (GRUs) are two variants that target RNN gradient and long-term memory problems. Yoon et al. proposed a novel Time-Series Generative Adversarial Network (TimeGAN) framework that combines GAN and RNN structures with a combination of supervised and unsupervised learning to account for temporal correlations (Yoon et al., 2019). The framework consists of an autoencoder and an adversarial network containing a sequence generator and a sequence discriminator. In dealing with the GAN variant with the imbalanced fault time series as the object, Shi et al. (2023) combine the loss function of WGAN-gp with TimeGAN, which effectively solves the problem of fault data scarcity. The architecture of GRU as a generator and discriminator in WGAN is improved (Zhang et al., 2023b), and the LSTM structure is used in references (Liu et al., 2022b; Zhao et al., 2022; Du et al., 2023a), in which Cycle-GAN or BiGAN is used as the basis.

## TransformerGAN

The core of Transformer is a self-attention mechanism, which is suitable for parallelized computing and offers higher accuracy and performance than RNNs and CNNs. The Transformer uses the self-attention mechanism to capture the correlation between time steps, effectively handling long-term dependencies from a global perspective. TTS-GAN (Li et al., 2022d) introduced Transformer, which works well to generate time series of any length similar to the original sequence. Both the generator and discriminator of the TTS-GAN model are built using Transformer. Xiang et al. proposed a Bayesian gated Transformer model for predicting the remaining service life of aero-engines. This model integrates a gating mechanism with the Transformer architecture to enhance the remote prediction of time series problems. The combination improves the network’s computational efficiency and its ability to represent short-term patterns (Xiang et al., 2024). When diagnosing the fault of the aerophysical unit, the Transformer structure is used in the generator when the WGAN model is used as the basic structure (Yan et al, 2023), while the discriminator still adopts the fully connected structure. Yan et al. (2024) also use a combination of such architectures and integrates Transformer with WGAN to train the model in combination with reinforcement learning. In some literatures (Fu et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024a), both generator and discriminator use Transformer to help cope with complex sample distributions in both generating fault samples and discriminating fault features. Among them, Fu et al. (2024) combine Transformer with ACGAN and Zhang et al. (2024a) combine Transformer with CGAN.

## 4.2.2 GAN networks process extracted features

In comparison to directly learning high-dimensional and intricate category features from the original time series data, traditional empirical analysis methods involve extracting statistical features. These include maximum and minimum values, variance, skewness, and kurtosis from the time series. Employing a manual feature extractor serves to diminish sample dimensionality while retaining essential feature information to the maximum extent (Jiang et al., 2019). Some studies (Zhou et al., 2020; Pan et al., 2021) utilize an encoder composed of neural networks as a feature extractor, enabling the learning of typical features inherent in the original sequence information. Additionally, the wavelet packet transform is applied to convert the original signal into the Hilbert space, facilitating the decomposition of features across different frequencies. In the case of (Lee et al., 2017), the Hilbert–Huang transform is employed to obtain distinctive energy spectral characteristics through empirical mode decomposition. Gao et al. (2024a) integrate a physical model embedded within a GAN. Interpretable features are extracted using a feature extraction network and then used as input to the generator. Additionally, some methods monitor oil chromatography data to detect abnormal gases produced during power transformer failures, recording these as characteristic values indicating the type of failure (Zhang et al., 2024c).

## 4.2.3 GAN’s processing of image data

An image can be regarded as a matrix array, where a grayscale image is represented as a one-dimensional matrix, while a color image is represented as a three-dimensional matrix, corresponding to the three color channels of R (red), G (green), and B (blue). Each channel is quantified by values ranging from 0 to 255, representing the color brightness. This numerical representation enables the conversion between the image and numerical data, essentially treating the image as continuous data.

## Process the collected Images

In industrial settings, images of faults and damages are captured using image sensors, physical quantity conversion imaging, or direct camera imaging. However, the available number of images representing these faults or abnormalities is limited. To address this issue, GAN-based oversampling technology is employed to augment the dataset by generating additional image data. For instance, GANs has been applied to produce highly realistic simulated wafer defect maps (Wang et al., 2019), and the style transfer capability of CycleGAN is utilized to transform the infrared image of a normal casing into an infrared representation of a faulty casing (Wei et al., 2019). Also using infrared thermography, Guo et al. (2024b) use non-contact infrared thermography to detect imbalanced rolling bearing defects. Some industrial devices require flawless surfaces, necessitating timely detection of texture, grain, and flatness. To address this, a method for recognizing defects in magnetic tiles using a fuzzy clustering generative adversarial network is proposed (Wang & Xue, 2024).

Different data forms have different characteristics, and the time-domain data, frequency-domain data, and image data processed by GAN are relatively high-dimensional, which generally occupies a large amount of computing resources. Frequency domain data is a spectral representation of a time-domain signal, which has more obvious attribute characteristics but may lose some time-domain information. Time-frequency domain data provides a two-dimensional representation of time series, allowing the application of various image processing techniques. However, this transformation may result in the loss of some sequential features inherent in the original time series. Conversely, time domain data is the most primitive data, and the data is complex and diverse but contains the most complete information, some deep hidden features sometimes cannot be extracted, therefore, the network structure needs to be optimized to mine important hidden information. Using two-dimensional convolution as the basic structure of GANs is more suitable for image data and better at representing fault characteristics. While, employing one-dimensional convolution or RNN as the foundational structure is more appropriate for time-domain or frequency-domain data. This approach preserves the original features, albeit in a less intuitive manner. In general, GANs have a wide range of application potential when dealing with different data forms, but which data form and GAN architecture to adopt depends on the specific task requirements and available data resources. For each data form, you need to weigh the model’s performance, data requirements, compute resources, and mission goals to determine the most appropriate approach. Additionally, GANs exhibit significantly higher computational complexity and resource requirements compared to some machine learning models, primarily due to their intricate adversarial training process. This makes them particularly suitable for addressing imbalances in complex and highdimensional data. Among deep generative models, VAEs are faster but produce lower-quality samples, while diffusion models generate higher-quality images but have longer sampling times, making sample generation significantly slower than with GANs and VAEs.

## 4.3 General approach and special strategies of GAN for fault diagnosis in industrial scenario

Traditional mechanical fault diagnosis methods rely heavily on signal processing capabilities and often exhibit poor model generalization. These methods typically analyze collected machine vibration data to detect anomalies. The imbalance in anomaly vi bration data can result in high misclassification costs for the corresponding fault categories. The fault diagnosis method based on GAN network is used to construct a small-sample training set of fault signals for GAN training, which simulates the generated data close to the real sample and balances the overall sample training set. Then, machine learning technology or neural network classifier is used to learn fault characteristics from the balanced sample set, and finally the model can accurately identify different fault categories and fault severity, so as to play a good role in health monitoring and early warning in machine operation. A typical solution to the problem of fault imbalance is to learn the mapping between the noise distribution and the real mechanical transient vibration data distribution by using GANs to learn the mapping relationship between the noise distribution and the real mechanical transient vibration data distribution, and further expand the available dataset (Zhang et al., 2020b). Since GAN is equivalent to a black box, it is impossible to accurately control the quality of the generated samples, and even welltrained GAN models may exhibit abnormal conditions, resulting in generated samples that can contain noise, uneven distribution, and varying quality. Therefore, the quality of the sample needs to be evaluated and screened. For image data, some commonly used image similarity metrics can be used, while for time series, similarity measures specific to time series or transformations into frequency domain features may be utilized. Different evaluation mechanisms are designed according to the type of data. To assess the effectiveness of generative models, normalized crosscorrelation and Kullback-Leibler divergence indicators are used (Rathore & Harsha, 2022), along with Jensen–Shannon divergence and root mean square error (Zhang et al., 2021b; Luo et al., 2022b). Using four indices: Initial Score, Fréchet Inception Distance, Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio, and Structural Similarity to quantitatively evaluate the generated images, a method was designed to fuse the evaluation screening strategy with data augmentation to facilitate the fitting of the generated samples to the real data distribution (Zhang et al., 2022d). Compared with a single indicator, a comprehensive multi-indicator assessment framework is established (Fan et al., 2022). Of course, some visualization methods such as tdistribution-random proximity embedding (Chae et al., 2024) can also be compared superficially. Some methods of screening samples will also have an impact on the subsequent training of classification models, a data screening module based on maximum mean difference (MMD) was designed in (Hou et al., 2023) to filter out similar data, Pearson correlation coefficient screening criterion (PCCSC) was proposed to ensure the quality of the generated samples (Li et al., 2022c), similar to single-index screening, some methods use multiple numerical indicators, and the data filter in reference (Liu et al., 2022c) is based on three indicators: discriminant probability KL. divergence, MMD Discriminant results categorical results, and correlation analysis results are used (Zhao & Yuan, 2021). More particularly, a convolutional variational encoder was designed (Shang et al., 2023) to learn the reconstruction loss of the original data first, and if the reconstruction loss of the false sample was greater than this term, it was omitted, and in (Shao et al., 2023b), the solitary forest (IF) algorithm was used to calculate the sample score, and the one with the high score was deleted as an anomaly. The above screening methods are all posttraining screening, which is in-training (Liu et al., 2023b), which uses a high-quality sample screening algorithm to select highquality fake samples generated by the generator and send them to the discriminator.

Following data augmentation with GANs, the purpose is to balance the quantitative relationship of the original dataset. The balanced data is then used for training classifiers, such as CNN (Meng et al., 2022), SVM (Pu et al., 2022), random forest (Pu et al., 2023a), decision tree, and others. CNN-based classifiers are often enhanced to better accommodate the classification tasks for different types of data. These classifiers can be designed to identify and classify the original time-domain data, transformed spectra, and various feature maps. Network structures such as CNN, LSTM, Transformer, and other structures can also be used in classifiers based on neural networks.

Some novel improvements have made GANs more effective in fault diagnosis. Zhang et al. (2022e) proposed an adaptive decoupling strategy, which adaptively obtains latent vectors from different distributions through a gradient inverse process to realize the decoupling of different bit distributions in the input. Liu et al. proposed a regularization method to adjust the accuracy of the discriminator and maintain the effective information gradient by regularizing the mutual information between the input information and the internal features of the discriminator (Liu et al., 2022c). These two methods effectively promote the stability of the model. Wang et al. developed a gradient flow-based meta-learning technique to improve the convergence of the model (Wang et al., 2023f), while Li et al. proposed to construct a meta-learning GANs with a self-supervised strategy, and these meta-learning-based GAN models can generate better pseudo-samples with a small number of samples (Li et al., 2023a). Zhuo & Ge (2021) and Ren et al. (2023b) proposed a few-shot learning GAN to solve the prob lem of few fault samples. Ren et al. (2023b; Wang et al., 2023d) and Liu et al. (2023a) simultaneously use the information of multiple domains to train the GAN model, so as to obtain more realistic and diverse data. Luo et al. used a two-stage GAN (2S GAN) structure (Luo et al., 2022a), in which the first-level GAN used TimeGAN to extract the time series information in the time series, and the second-level GAN used the supervised learning feature of ACGAN to enhance the information extraction ability. Ding et al. first used the K-nearest neighbor method (KNN) and undersampled the majority of the classes in the disequilibrium sample set, and then oversampled the minority classes using the improved TACKAN model based on ACGAN (Ding et al., 2022). Wang et al. used a mix ture of SMOTE and GAN to generate samples to increase sample size and diversity (Wang et al., 2023a). In the method mentioned by Gao et al. (2022a), numerical simulation is used to obtain sim ulated fault samples first, and then input these simulated samples into the GAN network together with real samples to generate more fault samples, which greatly improves the accuracy of sub sequent classification.

GAN-based fault diagnosis technology has demonstrated promising results across various application scenarios, as depicted in Fig. 8. Of course, some studies have integrated GAN with other methods or applied it in some special industrial scenarios.

## Transfer learning

Transfer learning-based methods are also widely used in the field of fault diagnosis (Li et al., 2024c; Tang et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2024), but some methods combine GAN data augmentation with transfer learning, using CycleGAN (Wang et al., 2023e), or using DCGAN (Zhong et al., 2023) to augment the data first, and then use transfer learning to train the model, unlike them, He et al. (2022) and Li et al. (2022a) combine WGAN and TimeGAN. Cui et al. (2024) used GANs to augment the original data, using the augmented data as the source for transfer learning. The paper proposes that after initially training the CNN with the hybrid data, the model undergoes secondary training with the source data. This secondary training enables the model to effectively diagnose both composite and single faults. Regarding the domain adaptation problem in transfer learning, the domain adversarial neural network (DANN) employs an adversarial approach to transfer source domain features to the unlabeled target domain. DANN consists of a feature extractor, a domain discriminator, and a classifier. The domain discriminator assists the feature extractor in learning domain-invariant features, while the classifier performs the classification. Liu et al. used DANN to transfer features from simulated data to achieve highprecision diagnosis (Liu & Gryllias, 2022). Similarly, Wang et al.

## Some specific application scenarios

The most common application scenario for GAN-based fault diagnosis is inter-plant mechanical instruments GAN-based faults is inter-plant mechanical instruments (Zhang et al., 2021d). For instance, several methods (Li et al., 2021d; Zhang et al., 2022f; Du et al., 2023b) use different GAN models to generate minority fault data to address the issue of insufficient fault data in HVAC systems. Furthermore, Hu et al. (2023) combined the concept of digital twins with a WGAN-based model to achieve highfidelity virtual sample modeling using only healthy physical samples from different industrial equipment. This approach effectively supports multiple server-side tasks, including early fault detection, health monitoring, and degradation follow-up. The WD-CGAN proposed in (Chen et al., 2023) is used to enhance the fault data generated by the aircraft’s electrostatic actuator (EHA) and classify the faults with SDAE, providing a method for the EHA fault diagnosis of the aircraft. Nuclear power plants operate under normal conditions most of the time, and the equipment is almost always operated in harsh environments such as high temperature, high pressure, and radioactivity, and once the machinery and equipment appear to be aged, damaged, and degraded performance, it will cause extremely serious harm, A lightweight CGAN has been studied (Qian & Liu, 2022), which can be used with a variety of classifiers to produce good diagnostic effects of nuclear power plant equipment. For diagnosing PV (Photovoltaic) array faults with finite small labeled samples, WGAN is used to learn various data distributions in PV systems (Lu et al., 2023), so that CNN classifiers can be trained with the generated labeled samples for diagnosis, providing a feasible method for the stable operation of PV power plants. Fault diagnosis based on traditional machine learning cannot train a model with strong generalization performance for diagnosing arc faults, so the improved GAN model is used to generate fault data of carbonization path arc in low-voltage cables (Zhang et al., 2021a), so that the diagnostic model can identify arc faults more effectively. In addition to this, in a real industrial environment. GANs have also been successfully applied to equipment failure prediction, manufacturing process optimization, and quality inspection. Wang et al. analyzed a real quality record database from a compressor manufacturer in China and utilized the CWGAN-XGBoost method (Wang et al., 2024b), achieving an improvement in pre-factory quality assessment accuracy of 3.77% and 3.12%, respectively. Gao et al. (2024b) used real data from offshore oil and gas field operations as experimental samples. Facing the challenge of diagnosing electric submersible pump faults due to scarce valid samples, they combined CGAN to generate the necessary samples, significantly enhancing diagnostic outcomes. Similarly, for the fault diagnosis and detection of common bearings, gears, tools, and other components, GANs demonstrated superior performance in both artificial damage experiments and real damaged parts (Fu et al., 2024; Quan et al., 2024). Furthermore, Wang et al. validated the effectiveness and practical efficacy of their GAN-based approach across eight datasets, including those from the semiconductor, electrical, and power grid sectors (Wang et al., 2024c). These findings illustrate the tremendous potential of GANs for fault diagnosis in real industrial scenarios and highlight the extensive prospects for further exploration.

## 5. Problems and Interpretability of GAN Method Under Small-Sample Mixing Imbalance

## 5.1 A review of several imbalance modes based on GAN resampling

Machines and equipment encounter noise pollution during data collection from sensors, stemming not only from ambient noise but also from the interference among various fault vibration signals, and the problem of mislabeling of sample labels caused by various reasons. When interference noise affects clean samples, or pure noise samples are present in the dataset, the GANgenerated samples may exhibit unreasonable fault features, causing a significant deviation from the real situation. To address environmental noise, an effective method involves preprocessing the data before model training. Yang et al. (2022a) use a high-pass filter to remove low-frequency components from the vibration signal and then converts the one-dimensional time series into a grayscale map, significantly reducing low-frequency noise interference and enabling accurate fault diagnosis. Additionally, converting the time series into the frequency (Sun et al., 2024) or timefrequency (Zhang et al., 2023a) domain using FFT, STFT, or CWT helps filter noise within specific frequency ranges while preserving useful signal components. To manage vibration signals with different frequencies, a progressive generation adversarial network is proposed (Ren et al., 2024), which gradually generates complex signals from low-frequency to wide-frequency bands. This method decomposes signals into different frequency bands and employs multiple GAN models to focus on these bands, avoiding high-energy signal interference with fault eigenfrequencies. Regarding noisy labels, Huang et al. designed an improved label noise-robust auxiliary classifier generative adversarial network (rAC-GAN) (Huang et al., 2021). Driven by limited data and incorporating regularized mutual information, this network exhibits higher robustness to label noise. In addition to the above data containing noise imbalances, a fault diagnosis approach for planetary gearboxes, employing SDAE-GAN (Wang et al., 2018), enhances limited samples through a GAN network. This method introduces noise with statistical characteristics to the samples and utilizes coding and decoding processes to derive noise-free samples. This approach facilitates the automated extraction of effective fault characteristics and enhances resilience to noise. To evaluate performance under different signal-to-noise ratios (Cao et al., 2022), a designed noise device is employed to introduce noise, followed by leveraging the GAN structure to generate adversarial samples, thereby elevating the model’s diagnostic accuracy. Additionally, a soft threshold method is applied to nonlinearly transform the signal (Zhang et al., 2023a), effectively reducing noise in the sig nal. These methods for handling noise offer valuable insights for subsequent research on addressing noise imbalance in diagnostic procedures.

In addressing imbalance in fault diagnosis, the objective of the data augmentation method is to facilitate the classifier in acquiring comprehensive information concerning both intra- and inter-class boundaries from the augmented dataset. Several pertinent studies (Li et al., 2021d; Wang et al., 2022a; Zhang et al., 2022e; Tan et al., 2023), have predominantly focused on mitigating inter-class imbalance. Specifically, several studies enhance the distribution balance among classes by leveraging both unlabeled and labeled data, incorporating a designed semi-supervised self-training scheme (Li et al., 2021d). To address inter-class imbalance, a conditional GAN is applied, effectively mitigating the imbalance between the majority of normal samples and multiple abnormal samples (Zhang et al., 2023a). For intra-class imbalance, the ACWGAN-GP model is introduced to handle fault data under diverse tooth-breaking conditions (Li et al., 2024b), Postdimension reduction, the generated sample data demonstrates significant inter-class separation and intra-class aggregation. It is noted that comprehensive improvement of generated data necessitates addressing the data distribution skewness introduced by GAN, particularly in ensuring the separability of intra-class features within the range of different inter-class features. This factor significantly influences sample diversity in the GAN generation process. Although (Ren et al., 2023a) proposed a method to provide sample distribution examples for underrepresented classes using classes with a surplus of samples, the coexistence of intraclass and multi-class imbalance in the context of small samples remains challenging. Firstly, the limited number of samples impedes the acquisition of fine-grained feature information. Secondly, some intra-class aggregations and other intra-class aggregations may overlap, posing a challenge for GAN-based resampling in establishing a valid boundary between the two.

Currently, the majority of research on fault diagnosis in the context of imbalanced time series predominantly focuses on short-term imbalanced fault data. Notably, there is a significant gap in exploring long-term imbalanced data. This gap primarily arises due to the extensive time required for data collection during prolonged failures. A potential solution involves accelerating the fault evolution process and subsequently mapping the accelerated data back to normal evolution data. To address this, GANs were employed to perform the imputation function for missing data, facilitating the restoration of long-term continuous structural responses within multi-channel spatiotemporal information (Gao et al., 2022b). The objective is to generate a continuation of the original signal, forming a new signal (Dai et al., 2023b).

Furthermore, addressing the imbalance in time series is pivotal in the research pertaining to the prediction of remaining life, Huang et al. (2022) and Zhang et al. (2022) both utilized GANs to simu late degradation scenarios of aero engines under variable work ing conditions and various failure modes, achieving Residual Life Prediction. Similarly, in response to the challenge of relying on an extensive amount of training data for bearing life prediction, Lu et al. proposed a training strategy that integrates the GAN method with a health predictor (Lu et al., 2022). This strategy generates prediction data utilizing only limited time series degradation data, thereby enhancing the performance and accuracy of the predictor.

## 5.2 Interpretability and privacy protection of GAN for fault diagnosis

The interpretability of artificial intelligence is rooted in the user’s trust in the system, as well as their ability to comfortably con trol and utilize it (Vilone & Longo, 2021). In deep learning, interpretability encompasses key concepts such as operability, provability, transparency, and post-causal interpretation. For instance, Dong et al. used GANs to generate renewable energy scenario data that aligns with the distribution of historical data (Dong et al., 2022). They employed interpretable features such as average power, maximum power, and minimum power, leveraging latent spatial vectors to govern the generation process. To further enhance interpretability, a physical model was incorporated into the integrated diagnostic framework. This addition facilitated the extraction of crucial features with increased transparency (Yan et al., 2023). Despite these efforts, existing studies on the interpretability of GANs remain inadequate for a comprehensive understanding of the entire GAN data augmentation system. In particular, in scenarios with small sample imbalance, interpretability should also be examined from various perspectives.

## 5.2.1. Category decision boundaries

Data distributions under mixing disequilibrium no longer have explicit class boundaries, and intra- and inter-class aggregation patterns need to be dissected in detail and how they affect the class characteristics and quality of the generated samples. For noisy samples, what specific ways are used by the model to reject or marginalize noisy clusters.

## 5.2.2. Model structure and weighting parameters

The internal operation mechanism of the GAN model is very important, the internal coding of the generator and the quantitative discrimination approach of the discriminator affect the adversarial training process, especially for potential vectors in the low-dimensional hidden space, which need to be made explicit to provide information about the mapping between them and the feature results.

## 5.2.3. Sample form and feature importance

The initial samples’ sample form and category statistics encapsulate all data characteristics and feature information. Through dimensionality reduction, various features can be further delineated. Model-independent techniques, such as Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations, SHapley Additive exPlanations, and others, offer a means to quantify both the contribution of each feature and the extent of the model’s dependence on these features.

GANs may also be involved in privacy and data breaches when generating and processing data. This often happens with personally identifiable information, medical records, etc., and to mitigate these issues, data needs to be handled with care, and one possible solution is to add noise to the original data or generate highly generalized data. In the field of fault diagnosis, a GAN with class information is introduced into a multi-client feature alignment module with adversarial learning to adaptively generate a reference distribution (Wang et al., 2022c), so as to construct a global fault diagnosis model for multiple clients under the condition of data privacy. This proposes a novel processing idea, but data protection based on GAN method still needs further research in the field of fault diagnosis.

## 6. Conclusions and Future Works

In industrial production practice and system maintenance, fault monitoring of some machinery and equipment relies on classification and diagnosis models trained on large-scale operation data. However, it is impractical to collect operational data of machinery under prolonged failure modes. GAN-based techniques for addressing small-sample imbalances effectively address this challenge. Nonetheless, various GAN methods differ in their application, principles, structures, and training processes. This paper, therefore, provides a comprehensive review of these methods from multiple perspectives.

This paper begins by introducing the characteristics of imbalanced data under small samples, elucidating the challenges associated with various categories of imbalanced samples. It then outlines the basic principle and network structure of GAN and some GAN variant modes and their improvements on the basic GAN. Notably, it highlights the exceptional capability of GANs as a specialized oversampling technology for sample generation. Subsequently, the paper delves into the industrial fault diagnosis field, discussing the data forms, network structures, principles, and application objects of GAN-based techniques for processing imbalanced fault data. It also examines the research status and limitations of various GAN methods under mixed and blended imbalance modes. The whole review characterizes the application of existing GAN techniques for fault diagnosis across different scenarios and provides an overview of the current state of GAN and its variants.

The challenge in imbalanced fault diagnosis techniques lies in mitigating recognition bias inherent in traditional machine learning models, primarily due to insufficient fault samples in certain classes. Moreover, the skewed data distribution complicates the establishment of effective decision boundaries. The imbalanced fault dataset further exacerbates the issue, given the high acquisition costs or scarcity of fault samples under restrictive conditions, leading to potential neglect or misclassification of critical issues in practical applications. Typical remedies encompass data-level approaches such as over-sampling, under-sampling, mixed sampling, and data generation methods (including VAEs, GANs, and Diffusion models). Algorithmic solutions involve weighting loss functions to prioritize minority class samples during model training, as well as integrated learning via ensemble methods combining multiple models and techniques. In actual industrial scenarios, effective solutions increasingly necessitate a blend of these methods, as singular approaches often fall short. GAN’s straightforward architecture facilitates seamless integration with diverse methodologies, significantly enhancing outcomes across network structure, loss function optimization, data preprocessing, and feature mapping. Therefore, an exploration of GAN’s application in imbalanced fault diagnosis offers insights for future research and methodological synergies.

GAN is a kind of generative model, its fundamental property is to learn the probability distribution of the original sample and finally simulate the convergent samples that meet the characteristics of the probability distribution of the original sample. Currently GAN is widely used in the field of mechanical fault diagnosis. It has a better ability to extract deeper features of the sample compared to the traditional sampling method. By adopting different network structures and combining some classifiers with efficient classification ability, GAN-based fault diagnosis methods play a key role in industrial equipment condition monitoring, operation and maintenance, life prediction and prevention of safety accidents. An overview of GAN network-based fault diagnosis techniques can provide references for many application areas in industry, data processing related fields, and for the design of specific equipment fault diagnosis models. Based on the above review, the following outlines the future research prospects and directions for GANs in the field of fault diagnosis from several perspectives:

## 6.1. From the level of data distribution scenarios

Existing GAN-based fault diagnosis methods are dealing with a single fault signal data after the classification task, the reality of the mechanical system failure accompanied by extreme imbalance, especially in: the data contains a large amount of noise, fault state data clusters scattered distribution, class overlap, etc., notably, there is a dearth of research on the synchronous integration of multi-source, multi-failure datasets under complex conditions in fault diagnosis involving mixed data. This gap in the literature is especially pertinent to addressing the complexities arising from the simultaneous presence of multi-failure modes, multi-class imbalance, and multi-class imbalance issues, as mentioned earlier. Investigating solutions to these challenges is crucial for comprehensively addressing the complexity introduced by variable conditions associated with multi-failure modes, making the study of data equalization under such circumstances immensely valuable.

## 6.2. From the application object

The operation and maintenance of high-end equipment, particularly in extreme service conditions, necessitate prompt fault diagnosis and abnormal state monitoring, facing challenges such as response latency, high computational resource consumption, lack of labels for short-term anomalous data, and noise interference, solutions may involve employing pre-trained ensemble GAN models. These models can utilize online learning and distributed computing, alongside efficient hardware acceleration methods, to enable real-time updating and inference. Additionally, GAN models can leverage semi-supervised or self-supervised learning approaches to reduce reliance on annotated data. By analyzing device data streams, GANs models prove valuable in analyzing equipment data flows, learning normal operating state distributions, and identifying abnormalities. However, some operational equipment encounters challenges in data acquisition, and actual data often contains noise, complicating the training of accurate and timely diagnostic models with limited data. GANs find application in the realm of digital twins, addressing data gaps by interpolating and enhancing missing data, thereby creating more precise digital twins. Through the generation of experimental and operational data in a virtual environment, GANs contribute to predictive maintenance, performance monitoring, and risk detection. Additionally, considering the confidentiality of operation data from crucial equipment and instruments, GANs-based data enhancement methods offer a solution to mitigate the risk of data leakage during mining. However, challenges persist, such as addressing the substantial differences between the enhanced data and the source data.

## 6.3. From the point of view of GAN’s own mechanism

GANs, as intricate deep models, exhibit a highly complex internal operational mechanism. The inherent black-box nature of GANs renders their training process challenging to precisely control, current challenges include poor interpretability, difficulties in accurately elucidating the mapping relationship from latent space to generated data, and discerning differences between real and generated data. Balancing the quality and diversity of generated samples presents a significant challenge. The impact of model parameters on training is pronounced, exacerbating difficulties, particularly with limited sample sizes, where issues like training instability and mode collapse loom large. Future research should focus on innovative regularization techniques to stabilize training, leveraging neural network models with fewer parame ters for GANs’ foundational structure to reduce computational complexity. Methods such as reinforcement learning and evolutionary computation can further enhance the training process and improve generation quality. Given increasingly complex ap plication contexts and diverse data modalities, investigating finegrained conditional generation methods and GAN architectures capable of handling multi-modal data is imperative.
