# 2. Related Work

## 2.1 Deep learning for chemical-process fault diagnosis
Research on the Tennessee Eastman process (Downs and Vogel, 1993) has become the proving ground for data-driven FDD. Convolutional models transform windowed multivariate data into two-dimensional tensors so that spatial and temporal structure can be learned jointly (Wu and Zhao, 2018). Extended deep belief networks concatenate raw signals with learned features to avoid discarding information during layer-wise compression (Wang et al., 2019). Process-topology convolutional networks encode plant connectivity as a graph and gain both accuracy and a measure of explainability (Wu et al., 2021). Recurrent and temporal-convolutional models, often paired with generative augmentation, target the incipient faults 3, 9 and 15 that defeat simpler classifiers (Lomov et al., 2021). These works define the state of the art on closed-set, balanced, simulated data, the setting this paper is designed to move beyond. Classical statistical monitoring based on principal component analysis remains the reference baseline and a source of interpretable detection statistics such as Hotelling's T² and the squared prediction error (Qin, 2003; Ji and Sun, 2022).

## 2.2 Class imbalance and incipient faults
Because faults are rare and incipient faults are weak, several studies synthesize minority samples with generative adversarial networks to rebalance training data. A recurring finding is that the majority (normal) class is easily overwhelmed when imbalance corrections are too aggressive. In this work we adopt a focal objective (Lin et al., 2017), which reweights hard examples without discarding the majority class, and we test it under an explicitly imposed imbalance rather than on the natively balanced benchmark.

## 2.3 Uncertainty quantification and open-set recognition
Softmax confidence is a biased signal for whether an input is out-of-distribution (Liu et al., 2020). Evidential deep learning places a Dirichlet distribution over the class probabilities and returns an explicit uncertainty mass in a single forward pass (Sensoy et al., 2018). Energy-based scores further separate in- and out-of-distribution inputs (Liu et al., 2020), and distance-based scores in a network's feature space, above all the Mahalanobis detector of Lee et al. (2018), are frequently the strongest of all. The related problems of anomaly, novelty, open-set, and out-of-distribution detection have been unified into a single taxonomy (Yang et al., 2022), and large-scale cross-benchmarking shows that scores sensitive to feature magnitude are the most stable across settings (Wang et al., 2024). Our open-set component follows this evidence: we score unknown faults with a feature-space Mahalanobis distance and report, as a finding in its own right, that softmax-derived scores are near chance on this task.

## 2.4 Transfer learning and the simulation-to-real gap
Domain adaptation for chemical-process FDD has been benchmarked on Tennessee Eastman under changing operating conditions, and non-stationarity (concept drift) is recognized as a safety-critical driver of silent model degradation (Hinder et al., 2023), with model confidence proposed as an unsupervised drift signal (Lobo et al., 2023). A structural obstacle is that a simulated benchmark and a real pilot plant seldom share a sensor space, which makes weight transfer ill-posed. We therefore report sim-to-real transfer honestly as a negative result and, separately, demonstrate that the same architecture succeeds when trained natively on real-plant data.

## 2.5 Soft sensing, control, and hybrid modelling
Adjacent to diagnosis, deep soft sensors predict product quality in refinery units (Sun and Ge, 2021; Yuan et al., 2020; Sifakis et al., 2023), reinforcement learning optimizes process operation (Devarakonda et al., 2025), and physics-informed hybrid models combat data scarcity and plant–model mismatch (Bangi et al., 2022). Real oil-and-gas studies confirm that data-driven diagnosis reaches operating equipment: event detection on production data (Qu et al., 2023) and centrifugal-pump fault prediction on real refinery data (Orrù et al., 2020). These provide the applied context for our framework; a refinery soft-sensor benchmark is included as auxiliary evidence rather than as the primary evaluation.

## 2.6 The gap this paper targets
The individual ingredients we use are established: the focal loss (Lin et al., 2017), feature-space Mahalanobis scoring (Lee et al., 2018), and threshold calibration are each known in isolation. Our contribution is not a new loss or a new score; it is the demonstration that a *single* diagnostic backbone can be made trustworthy for process-safety use by combining them, and the evidence for what such a combination does and does not deliver on a safety benchmark. Three things distinguish this from the existing chemical-process FDD literature. First, detection is reported at a stated false-alarm budget rather than as argmax accuracy, which is the metric an alarm-management system is actually tuned to (a point the closed-set Tennessee Eastman leaderboard ignores). Second, the same network rejects faults it was never trained on through a feature-space score, and we show, apparently for the first time on a chemical process, that softmax-based rejection is near chance while feature distance is not. Third, we quantify the honest boundaries of the approach, including a negative simulation-to-plant transfer result, rather than reporting only the favourable numbers. No prior study combines a calibrated, deployment-oriented operating point with open-set rejection and interpretability inside one deep model, evaluated under the canonical Tennessee Eastman protocol and cross-checked on a real petrochemical plant. Trustworthiness, meaning knowing when to raise an alarm, when to abstain, and why, rather than another increment in closed-set accuracy, is the contribution we pursue.
