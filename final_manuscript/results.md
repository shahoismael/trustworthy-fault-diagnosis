# 4. Results

## 4.1 Diagnosis on the canonical Tennessee Eastman benchmark
Under the canonical protocol (train on the training simulations, test on the separate testing simulations), the unified convolutional backbone reaches a macro-averaged F1 of 0.88 ± 0.06 across the known fault classes over five random seeds (95% CI ±0.05; individual seeds 0.80–0.92; Table 2). This is achieved on held-out test windows drawn from simulation runs the model never saw during training, so the number reflects genuine generalization rather than a re-split of the training data. For context, classical and shallow baselines on the development split range from a macro-F1 of 0.42 (PCA with nearest-centroid) through 0.43 (linear SVM) and 0.63 (random forest) to 0.71 (multilayer perceptron), and a plain convolutional network already exceeds them; the deep backbone's advantage over classical monitoring is therefore consistent with the wider literature (Wu and Zhao, 2018).

## 4.2 What the focal objective buys: incipient faults
The controlled ablation (Table 3) isolates the training objective on the same canonical test set. Plain cross-entropy attains a macro-F1 of 0.874 but effectively fails on the incipient faults, with recall of 0.00 on faults 9 and 15. Switching to the focal loss raises macro-F1 to 0.918 and lifts incipient-fault recall to 0.65 (fault 9) and 0.42 (fault 15), with fault 3 detected almost perfectly. Adding square-root class weighting trades a little macro-F1 (0.892) for a further gain in the rarest-fault recall. The practical reading is that the focal objective, not the class weighting, is the component that recovers the hard faults; weighting is a smaller lever used to shape the operating point. This recovery is real on average but unstable: across five seeds, incipient-fault recall is 0.73 ± 0.41 (fault 3), 0.79 ± 0.16 (fault 9) and 0.26 ± 0.24 (fault 15), so the hardest incipient fault remains only partially and inconsistently detected. We state this limitation plainly in §5.8 rather than average it away.

## 4.3 A deployable operating point
Reporting diagnosis accuracy alone would hide the behaviour that decides deployability. Using a naïve argmax rule, the model floods the normal class with false alarms because eighteen fault classes outvote one normal class. Calibrating the decision threshold on validation normal windows to a five-percent false-alarm budget (§3.5) fixes this: on the test set the model achieves a fault-detection rate of 0.90 at a false-alarm rate of 0.06, with a mean detection delay of 47.8 minutes (Table 4). This is the difference between a classifier and a monitor an operator would tolerate.

## 4.4 Recognizing unknown faults
Three faults (16, 17, 18) were withheld from training entirely and presented only at test time. The choice of rejection score is decisive (Table 5). Softmax-derived scores computed from the trained model are essentially useless here: maximum softmax probability, entropy, and energy give AUROC values of 0.43, 0.44 and 0.44, whereas the feature-space Mahalanobis distance separates unknown from known faults with an AUROC of 0.82. This corroborates the cross-benchmark finding that feature-magnitude-sensitive scores dominate softmax scores for open-set detection (Lee et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2024), now shown for a chemical process. Across five seeds the Mahalanobis open-set AUROC is stable at 0.845 ± 0.006.

## 4.5 The same network on a real plant
To show the framework is not a Tennessee-Eastman artefact, the identical architecture was trained as a supervised normal-versus-fault detector on real PRONTO pilot-plant data (17 continuous variables, time-ordered split). It reaches an accuracy of 0.977, a macro-F1 of 0.973, and an AUROC of 0.990 on held-out real-plant windows (Table 6). The backbone therefore transfers as an architecture across a simulated continuous process and a real multiphase-flow process.

## 4.6 Interpretability
Input-gradient attribution over the trained model ranks the reactor cooling-water temperature (xmeas_21) and the condenser cooling valve (xmv_10) as the most influential variables, followed by stripper and separator measurements (Table 7, Figure 4). These are physically coherent: the cooling and separation subsystems are where several Tennessee Eastman faults first manifest, so the model's attention aligns with the underlying fault mechanisms rather than with spurious correlations.

## 4.7 Auxiliary generalization evidence
The framework's components extend to adjacent fault-diagnosis settings, each reported with five-seed confidence intervals (Table 8). On the real refinery soft-sensor benchmark, time-lagged regression predicts the debutanizer quality variable with $R^2 = 0.994 \pm 0.001$ and the SRU H₂S concentration with $R^2 = 0.848 \pm 0.005$. On the metallurgical Steel Plates set, a seven-class classifier reaches a macro-F1 of $0.786 \pm 0.006$. On the IndPenSim fed-batch bioprocess, sample-level fault detection reaches an AUROC of $\approx 1.0$; we report this as a sanity check rather than a headline, since sample-level detection on this simulation is near-trivial (the fault rate is 2.6% and the signatures are strongly separable). These experiments use task-appropriate models and are presented as generalization evidence, not as the same convolutional backbone.

## 4.8 A negative result: simulation-to-real transfer
For completeness we tested transferring a detector from the simulated Tennessee Eastman process to the real PRONTO plant. Because the two do not share a sensor space, weight transfer is ill-posed; a method-level transfer (a reconstruction monitor established on simulated normal data and re-fit on real normal data) yields an AUROC of 0.44, and a few-shot calibrated variant reaches only 0.46, both at or below chance (Table 4). We report this openly. It does not contradict Section 4.5, which trains on real data; it shows that naïve cross-domain transfer without a shared representation is not a solved problem, which is a more useful message than a polished but misleading transfer number.

## 4.9 Summary
The picture is consistent. A single backbone diagnoses known faults, recovers incipient faults through the focal objective, rejects unknown faults through a feature-space distance, operates at a stated false-alarm budget, explains itself in physically sensible terms, and repeats its success on a real plant, while the one component that genuinely does not work, cross-domain transfer, is reported as such.
