# 6. Conclusion

We set out to make data-driven fault diagnosis for petrochemical processes trustworthy rather than merely accurate, and to do so with one model instead of a patchwork. A single one-dimensional convolutional backbone, trained with a focal objective, serves as the basis for multi-class diagnosis, incipient-fault detection, unknown-fault rejection, and interpretable attribution. On the canonical Tennessee Eastman benchmark it reaches a macro-F1 of 0.88 ± 0.06 over five seeds, recovers two of the three notoriously hard incipient faults (3 and 9) that plain cross-entropy misses while leaving the most subtle, fault 15, as an open problem, and, after calibrating its decision threshold on validation data, operates at a fault-detection rate of 0.90 with a false-alarm rate of 0.06 and a mean detection delay of about 48 minutes. A feature-space Mahalanobis score rejects three fully held-out unknown faults at an AUROC of 0.82, where softmax-based scores are no better than chance, and gradient attribution points to the cooling and separation variables that physically drive the faults. Trained natively on real PRONTO pilot-plant data, the same architecture detects real faults at an AUROC of 0.99, so the approach is not an artefact of one simulator.

We also report what does not work: transferring a detector from the simulator to the real plant, which have no shared sensor space, fails at or below chance. We keep this result in the paper because it points to a genuine open problem, cross-domain fault diagnosis with a shared representation, rather than papering over it.

For practice, three messages carry over directly. Report detection at a stated false-alarm budget, not argmax accuracy. Build unknown-fault rejection in feature space, not on the softmax. And separate what an architecture can learn on a plant from what a model can transfer between plants. Future work should pursue multi-seed canonical evaluation with rotated unknown-fault sets, physics-guided handling of low-signal incipient faults, and cross-domain benchmarks that give the simulation-to-real problem a fair chance to be solved.
